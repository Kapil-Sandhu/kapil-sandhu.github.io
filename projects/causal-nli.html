Natural Language Inference with Causal Language Models

Autoregressive 3-Way NLI Classification · Cross-Genre Multi-NLI Generalization · Adversarial Negation Robustness

An end-to-end empirical research framework implementing 3-way Natural Language Inference (entailment, neutral, contradiction) using decoder-only causal language models (Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B). Evaluates zero-shot vs supervised fine-tuned representations across in-domain classification (SNLI), cross-genre out-of-domain transfer (Multi-NLI), and adversarial hypothesis negation.

🎯 1. Problem Formulation & Task Definitions

Given a Premise ($P$) and a Hypothesis ($H$), the model predicts a categorical label $Y \in \{\text{entailment}, \text{neutral}, \text{contradiction}\}$ defined by formal propositional logic:

Entailment ($P \implies H$)

The hypothesis is necessarily true whenever the premise is true.

P: "A dog catching a frisbee."
H: "An animal is outside."
Neutral ($P \not\implies H \land P \not\implies \neg H$)

The truth value cannot be determined strictly from the premise alone.

P: "A boy jumping into a pool."
H: "The boy loves swimming."
Contradiction ($P \implies \neg H$)

The hypothesis is necessarily false given the premise.

P: "Two people walking down the street."
H: "Two people are sleeping in bed."

🔬 3. Detailed Comparative Analysis & Insights

📈 In-Domain SNLI Performance (+52.7% Jump)

Zero-shot base models hovered barely above random chance (35.55% on 0.6B and 41.29% on 1.7B) due to verbose generations and severe class imbalance. A single epoch of SFT on 50,000 pairs yielded an extraordinary jump to 88.25% and 90.43% with balanced Macro-F1 across all 3 classes.

🌐 Cross-Genre Domain Generalization (Multi-NLI)

While SNLI consists entirely of simple image caption descriptions, Multi-NLI introduces complex genres including government memos, telephone transcripts, Slate articles, and fiction. Models retained 79.41% (0.6B) and 83.83% (1.7B), proving the causal LM learned genuine semantic inference rather than SNLI lexical artifacts.

⚡ Adversarial Hypothesis Negation

Syntactically negating the main verb in the hypothesis (converting Entailment $\leftrightarrow$ Contradiction) revealed persistent lexical bias: models occasionally correlate the token "not" with contradiction regardless of context. Fine-tuning raised accuracy from 35.37% to 60.10% on 1.7B, highlighting the need for targeted adversarial data augmentation.

⚖️ Parameter Scaling (0.6B vs 1.7B)

Scaling parameter count by $2.8\times$ delivered consistent gains across every benchmark: SNLI (+2.18%), Multi-NLI Generalization (+4.42%), and Adversarial Negation (+5.04%). The larger model exhibited a substantially greater relative advantage on harder, out-of-domain, and adversarial distributions.

⚙️ 4. System Architecture & Training Configuration

Supervised Fine-Tuning Format

Autoregressive sequence template with strict end-of-turn delimiter:

Premis: <Premise>. Hypothesis: <Hypothesis>. Label: <entailment|neutral|contradiction><|im_end|>

Training Hyperparameters

  • Base Architectures: Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B & Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B
  • Training Split: 50,000 SNLI pairs (label -1 filtered)
  • Precision: bfloat16 + Gradient Checkpointing
  • Optimizer: AdamW ($\text{lr} = 2 \times 10^{-5}$, weight decay $0.01$)
  • Effective Batch Size: $8 \text{ (micro)} \times 4 \text{ (accum)} = 32$
  • Schedule: Cosine learning rate decay with 5% warmup over 1 epoch
  • Inference: Left-padding batched causal greedy generation (batch_size=16)
